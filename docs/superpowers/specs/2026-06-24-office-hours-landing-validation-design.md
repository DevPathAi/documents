# Design: DevPath AI pre-validation landing page

## Problem Statement

DevPath AI needs Round 2 evidence that is stronger than AI-generated personas and stronger than a generic waitlist. The current landing-page plan is useful, but only if its role is framed correctly: it does not prove demand by itself. It creates a named pool of potential beta users and interview candidates, then turns those users into evidence through concierge interviews, WTP follow-up, and later beta conversion.

The target user remains narrow: Korean junior developers, bootcamp graduates, backend job seekers, and self-taught learners who get stuck deciding what to study next and who repeatedly re-explain their current context to ChatGPT or search tools.

## Demand Evidence

Current evidence is still pre-product. The plan has a strong evidence design, not completed evidence.

- The MVP spec defines H1 as at least 5% paid conversion among 100 beta learners for a monthly 14,900 KRW package.
- The MVP spec defines H2 as LCS answers scoring at least +1.0/5.0 higher than context-free ChatGPT-style answers.
- The landing-page plan can support both, but only as an upstream source of named leads, interview subjects, and beta candidates.

Do not present waitlist count as demand. Present it as the top of the validation funnel.

## Status Quo

Users currently solve the problem with a messy mix of:

- ChatGPT prompts where they manually restate their level, current topic, stack, and error context.
- Google, blog posts, lectures, and GitHub examples stitched together without a clear next step.
- Bootcamp or community advice that helps sometimes, but does not remember the user's learning path.
- Personal uncertainty: "What should I study next?" and "Am I actually improving?"

The real competitor is not only another AI learning product. It is the user's current workaround: search, ChatGPT, lectures, and guessing.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

The narrowest wedge should be:

> Backend-oriented Korean junior or job-seeking developers learning Java/Spring/JPA who recently hit a concrete error or concept gap and had to explain their context repeatedly to get help.

The smallest paid-worthy version is not the full DevPath AI platform. It is a concierge-style "30-minute learning path diagnosis + context-aware answer comparison" session:

1. User submits current level, stack, goal, and one recent stuck moment.
2. Founder manually or semi-manually produces a short diagnosis and next 2-week study path.
3. User brings one real question or error.
4. Founder shows two answers: generic ChatGPT-style vs. context-aware DevPath-style.
5. User rates usefulness, clarity, and whether they would pay for this monthly.

This directly tests H1 and H2 before the full product exists.

## Constraints

- Landing-page budget should stay near zero because the AI solution budget is already mostly allocated to AskMind and Kallo.
- The landing must respect existing privacy direction: minimum collection, explicit consent, withdrawal path, and separate interview-recording consent.
- Landing WTP is a weak stated signal. Treat it as a screening clue, not price proof.
- The page should reuse existing design direction: Korean-first copy, Pretendard, restrained indigo/slate tokens, and no overbuilt marketing shell.
- The validation output must be useful for Round 2: named waitlist count, interview insights, LCS pain evidence, WTP patterns, and beta conversion plan.

## Premises

1. The landing page's primary purpose is not to prove demand. It is to create a named beta and interview pool.
2. WTP responses on the landing page are not price validation. They are screening and interview prompts.
3. Round 2 proof should emphasize interview insights and beta readiness more than waitlist vanity numbers.
4. The strongest early test is watching real learners explain a recent stuck moment and comparing generic vs. context-aware help.
5. The landing page should collect enough data to prioritize interviews, but not enough to create privacy drag or completion drop-off.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Minimal waitlist page

Build a static landing page with email, current stage, optional WTP, and interview opt-in.

Pros:
- Fastest to ship.
- Low cost and low technical risk.
- Good enough to start collecting named leads.

Cons:
- Produces weak evidence unless followed by disciplined interviews.
- Reporting and screening remain manual.
- Easy to overclaim waitlist count as demand.

### Approach B: Evidence pipeline page

Build the landing page with event tracking, lead scoring, structured Google Sheets fields, interview shortlist views, and a report template.

Pros:
- Converts page activity directly into Round 2 artifacts.
- Makes channel conversion, WTP response, and interview opt-in visible from day one.
- Reduces manual cleanup before making the validation PDF.

Cons:
- Slightly more implementation effort.
- Can become overbuilt if analytics work delays user conversations.
- Still does not prove demand without actual interviews and beta behavior.

### Approach C: Concierge-first validation

Keep the landing small and use it mainly to recruit 10 to 15 high-quality interviews and concierge sessions.

Pros:
- Fastest route to real user language and real pain.
- Directly tests whether LCS matters before full automation exists.
- Stronger than survey answers because users discuss actual recent behavior.

Cons:
- Lower waitlist volume.
- More founder time per participant.
- Requires discipline to capture transcripts, quotes, and ratings cleanly.

## Recommended Approach

Choose a hybrid of C + B: **Concierge-first validation with an evidence pipeline**.

Engineering review decision:

- The 48-hour smoke test should not start by implementing the full P7 Jaspr landing. P7 remains the long-term landing home, but the first validation pass uses a one-screen lightweight page/form so user conversations start before marketing infrastructure.
- The form backend should be Google Apps Script Web App + Google Sheets. Step 1 inserts a lead row; Step 2 updates the same row by `lead_id` or email. Formspree remains a backup if Apps Script deployment blocks the launch.

The page should remain simple, but every submission should flow into a structured validation sheet:

- Step 1: email, current stage, consent, source UTM.
- Step 2: stack, recent stuck moment, optional WTP, interview opt-in.
- Scoring columns: pain specificity, Java/Spring fit, WTP present, interview willingness, source quality.
- Interview pipeline: shortlisted, invited, scheduled, completed, paid, insight coded.
- Report tabs: waitlist summary, WTP distribution, interview themes, LCS evidence, beta conversion candidates.

Minimum lead schema:

- Identity: `lead_id`, `email_normalized`, `email_raw`.
- Consent: `consent_required`, `consent_version`, `consent_accepted_at`.
- Step timestamps: `step1_submitted_at`, `step2_submitted_at`, `last_updated_at`.
- Source: `utm_source`, `utm_medium`, `utm_campaign`, `utm_content`, `referrer`, `landing_variant`.
- Profile: `current_stage`, `stack`, `recent_stuck_moment`, `wtp_krw`, `interview_opt_in`.
- Scoring: `pain_specificity_score`, `spring_fit_score`, `source_quality_score`, `lead_score`.
- Interview pipeline: `status`, `shortlisted_at`, `invited_at`, `scheduled_at`, `completed_at`, `honorarium_paid_at`, `insight_coded_at`.

Submission behavior:

- Step 1 creates or updates a row by `lead_id`; if no `lead_id` is present, the backend falls back to `email_normalized`.
- Step 2 must update the existing row, not append a duplicate.
- Repeated submissions are idempotent: later non-empty values update the row, empty Step 2 fields do not erase prior non-empty values.
- Consent is versioned so later copy changes do not blur which wording the lead accepted.

Privacy guardrail:

- The smoke-test form must not collect source code, full stack traces, GitHub repository URLs, company confidential information, API keys, tokens, DB URLs, or long logs.
- The "recent stuck moment" field asks for a 1 to 2 sentence situation description only.
- The client and Apps Script backend should warn or reject obvious sensitive patterns such as `github.com`, `http`, `password=`, `token`, `BEGIN PRIVATE KEY`, `.env`, JDBC URLs, and long stack-trace-like multiline input.
- Real code, logs, and screen details move to the concierge session only after separate consent.

Required test matrix:

```
CODE PATHS / DATA PATHS                              USER FLOWS
[+] Smoke-test form                                  [+] Step 1 waitlist registration
  ├── [GAP] Valid Step 1 inserts one row               ├── [GAP] User submits email + stage + consent
  ├── [GAP] Missing consent is rejected                ├── [GAP] Duplicate click does not create 2 rows
  ├── [GAP] Invalid email is rejected                  └── [GAP] Network failure shows retryable error
  ├── [GAP] UTM/referrer/variant captured
  └── [GAP] Success shows Step 2 inline              [+] Step 2 profiling

[+] Apps Script upsert                                ├── [GAP] User completes stack + stuck moment
  ├── [GAP] Existing lead_id updates same row          ├── [GAP] Empty optional fields do not erase Step 1
  ├── [GAP] email_normalized fallback updates row      └── [GAP] Later Step 2 retry updates same row
  ├── [GAP] Empty Step 2 fields do not overwrite
  └── [GAP] consent_version persists                 [+] Privacy guard

[+] Sensitive-input guard                             ├── [GAP] Stack trace-like input is blocked/warned
  ├── [GAP] token/password/private-key patterns block  ├── [GAP] Normal Korean pain sentence passes
  ├── [GAP] URL/GitHub patterns block or warn          └── [GAP] User can edit and resubmit safely
  └── [GAP] Long multiline log-like input blocks

COVERAGE TARGET: 0/18 currently specified paths tested because implementation has not begun.
QUALITY TARGET: all listed paths must have either automated tests or a documented manual verification step before public sharing.
```

Submission state machine:

```
idle
  -> submitting_step1
  -> saved_step1
  -> profiling_step2
  -> submitting_step2
  -> saved_step2

Any submitting state
  -> failed_retryable
  -> submitting_step1 or submitting_step2
```

Rules:

- Do not show completion copy until the Apps Script response confirms the row was written.
- Store short in-progress form values in `localStorage` while a submission is pending or retryable.
- Clear `localStorage` after confirmed success.
- Never store blocked sensitive values in `localStorage`.
- Disable the submit button while a request is in flight to prevent duplicate rows.
- If Apps Script fails, show a retryable error and preserve the user's inputs.

The operating center is not the landing page. The operating center is the weekly validation review:

1. Review new leads.
2. Pick the 5 most specific pain cases.
3. Run concierge sessions.
4. Capture quotes and answer-comparison ratings.
5. Update the Round 2 evidence report.

## Open Questions

- Brand decision: DevPath AI only, DevPath AI with Korean alias, or 메아이 as the public-facing name.
- Exact first concierge offer: "30-minute interview" vs. "free learning path diagnosis" vs. "AI answer comparison session."
- Whether WTP should be asked on the landing page, after the concierge session, or both.
- Whether the first public CTA should emphasize beta access or paid research interview.

## Success Criteria

Green:
- 100+ named waitlist leads.
- 10 to 15 completed interviews or concierge sessions.
- At least 6 sessions where a real recent stuck moment is described in enough detail to evaluate LCS.
- At least 5 users rate context-aware answer materially better than generic answer.
- At least 20 high-fit beta candidates with Java/Spring or backend learning context.

Yellow:
- 50 to 100 named leads.
- 6 to 9 completed interviews.
- LCS pain appears, but users do not strongly describe it without prompting.
- WTP exists but clusters below the planned 14,900 KRW point.

Red:
- Fewer than 50 leads after warm-channel distribution.
- Fewer than 6 completed interviews.
- Users mainly want generic roadmap advice and do not care about context-aware answers.
- Interview participants cannot name recent concrete stuck moments.

## Distribution Plan

Distribution should prioritize warm, high-intent channels before broad community posting:

1. GitHub README top banners on active repos.
2. Inline CTA at the 50% scroll point in top technical articles.
3. Founder profile links and pinned posts.
4. Developer communities using the framing "junior developer learning interview" rather than product promotion.
5. Kallo and AskMind follow-up links for respondents who express interest.

Each channel must use UTM parameters. Channel success should be measured by high-fit interview completions, not only visits or waitlist registrations.

## NOT in Scope

- Full P7 Jaspr landing implementation. The smoke test produces copy/data for it, but does not implement the long-term landing shell.
- Payment integration or real paid conversion. WTP remains a stated signal and interview prompt.
- Collecting source code, GitHub repository analysis, stack traces, or full logs through the public form.
- Automated interview scheduling. Manual scheduling is enough for the first 5 to 15 sessions.
- Production CRM integration. Google Sheets is the operating database for this validation pass.
- Statistical proof of willingness to pay. Kallo/AskMind and later beta conversion handle stronger quantitative validation.

## Implementation Tasks

1. Create a one-screen smoke-test page with Korean CTA copy, required consent checkbox, Step 1 fields, Step 2 inline expansion, and retryable error/success states.
2. Implement Apps Script `doPost(e)` with JSON parsing, `lead_id`/`email_normalized` upsert, non-empty Step 2 update semantics, consent version storage, and JSON response bodies.
3. Create the Google Sheet tabs: `leads`, `interview_pipeline`, `report_waitlist`, `report_wtp`, `report_lcs_evidence`, `report_beta_candidates`.
4. Add client-side and Apps Script sensitive-pattern guards for code/log/token/GitHub URL submissions.
5. Add UTM/referrer/landing variant capture and preserve these values on Step 1 insert.
6. Add the submission state machine, in-flight button disabling, retry UI, and temporary `localStorage` preservation with success cleanup.
7. Test all paths in the required test matrix before sharing the link publicly.
8. After 5 concierge sessions, update hero copy and CTA wording from observed user language before moving the result into the P7 Jaspr landing.

## The Assignment

Before building the polished page, run a 48-hour concierge smoke test:

1. Publish or privately share a one-screen CTA asking for "30-minute learning path diagnosis for junior/backend learners."
2. Recruit 5 people from the warmest channels: GitHub readers, existing docs readers, acquaintances, developer communities where you already have trust.
3. In each session, ask for one recent stuck moment and capture the exact workaround.
4. Show generic vs. context-aware answer examples and collect a 1 to 5 usefulness rating.
5. After 5 sessions, decide the landing hero around the language users actually used, not the founder pitch.

This is the fastest way to avoid building a beautiful page around the wrong promise.
